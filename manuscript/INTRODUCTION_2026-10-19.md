# 1. Introduction

Written 19 October 2026 from `docs/REFERENCES.md` and the version controlled protocol. Two
annotations in that reference file assert claims this review has withdrawn, and the text below does
not use them; the discrepancy is recorded at the end of this file.

## 1.1 The question became answerable before it became answered

Whether a drug slows biological aging has until recently been a question without a measurable
endpoint in human trials. That changed for the incretin class when a randomised trial reported that
semaglutide modulates validated epigenetic aging clocks, the first randomised evidence that any
incretin based therapy moves a direct measure of biological age rather than a correlate of it.
Alongside it sits a broader argument that a small number of interventions now demonstrably move next
generation epigenetic clocks, and a survey of the human trials that have attempted it at all.

The consequence is specific and it defines the gap this review addresses. Evidence that one molecule
in a class moves a clock makes the class worth examining; it does not tell us which pharmacology is
responsible. The agents now in use differ from one another at the receptor, not merely at the dose.
Selective glucagon like peptide 1 receptor agonism, dual agonism adding the glucose dependent
insulinotropic polypeptide receptor, dual agonism adding the glucagon receptor, and triple agonism
engaging all three are four distinct pharmacologies, and no synthesis of aging relevant biomarkers
stratifies by that distinction. The two systematic reviews that pool inflammatory markers across
incretin trials cover mono agonists only, neither stratifies by receptor class, and one of them
states the omission explicitly and calls for the comparison attempted here.

## 1.2 Why weight loss is the wrong default explanation and the right null hypothesis

The field reads these drugs through weight. The reading is reasonable, because weight loss is large,
reliable and itself improves most of the biomarkers in question, and any claim of a weight
independent effect must survive adjustment for it. It is also incomplete, and four independent lines
of evidence indicate that something other than weight is operating.

In a cardiovascular outcomes trial of semaglutide, high sensitivity C reactive protein fell by 37.8
per cent, the reduction preceded weight loss in time, and it occurred in participants who lost no
weight. In a phase 2 trial of survodutide, improvements in insulin resistance and adiponectin were
described as largely independent of bodyweight change. In an analysis of hepatic treatment response,
weight loss accounted for 54 per cent of fibrosis improvement, which leaves 46 per cent unattributed
to any mechanism, and it is that residual the hepatic analysis of this review is built to
interrogate. Proteomic work in obesity and in metabolic dysfunction associated steatohepatitis
reports modulation of inflammatory and fibrotic pathways that is not obviously reducible to mass
loss.

None of this establishes a weight independent effect. Weight loss plausibly lies on the causal path
from receptor engagement to biomarker improvement, so conditioning on it removes part of the effect
being estimated, and an adjusted estimate is therefore a conservative lower bound rather than a
clean separation. The point is narrower: the residual is large enough, and reported often enough,
that the question of which receptor pharmacology produces benefit per unit of weight lost is a
quantitative question rather than a rhetorical one.

## 1.3 Aging is not a single direction, and one axis moves the wrong way

A composite that counted only favourable markers would mislead, because this class does not move
every aging domain favourably. Lean mass falls, and the magnitude has been framed as comparable to a
decade or more of aging. A network meta-analysis spanning 262 trials and 99,791 participants reports
the agent with the largest fat mass reduction in the class also producing the largest lean mass
reduction. Whether that loss is the adaptive accompaniment of fat loss or a maladaptive change with
consequences for frailty is precisely the ambiguity the available body composition data cannot
resolve, since the distinction requires function and not mass.

Any honest summary of this class across aging domains must therefore carry an unfavourable axis
alongside the favourable ones, and must report how many domains it is summarising. A score computed
over seven biomarkers is not commensurate with one computed over sixteen, and presenting them on the
same scale without that disclosure is the failure mode a composite invites.

## 1.4 What cannot be answered, stated before what can

Three absences bound this review, and naming them first is a condition of interpreting anything that
follows.

| Absence | Consequence |
|---|---|
| No epigenetic clock data exist for any dual or triple agonist, and no trial in this corpus reports an epigenetic clock for any agent | The premise that opened this introduction is evidence about one molecule. This review cannot extend it to receptor classes, and does not attempt to |
| Off treatment trajectory is published for body weight only | Nothing comparable exists for hepatic fat, inflammatory markers or epigenetic age, for any agent, which bounds any framework for discontinuation |
| Aging biomarkers appear in this literature predominantly in post hoc analyses rather than as prespecified endpoints | Selective reporting in that setting is not detectable by funnel asymmetry, because results never reported do not enter the literature as small imprecise studies |

The first of these is the sharpest. The inventory of this review declares an epigenetic age entry
and the corpus carries no contrast for it, a fact established by joining the declared inventory to
the extracted master rather than by assumption. The question that makes this class interesting in
aging terms is the question its trials have not measured.

## 1.5 What this review does

It asks which established aging biomarkers these therapies modify and in which direction; whether the
signature differs by receptor class; whether the hepatic signal with glucagon receptor containing
agents is associated with benefit beyond what weight loss explains; whether a composite can place
receptor classes on one scale; and what thresholds would define metabolic age normalisation
sufficiently to justify a structured discontinuation trial. The third of these is reported as an
association and not as a mechanism, which the trial level design requires: slopes estimated across
trials can differ in magnitude and in sign from relationships within patients, so every conclusion
here is framed about drug classes and never about individuals.

The thresholds of the fifth question are offered in the spirit of the 2021 consensus that defined
remission in type 2 diabetes before prospective trials had tested whether patients meeting the
definition experienced different long term outcomes. That definition was useful because it converted
an unstructured clinical question into a measurable one, and it carried provisional status while
doing so. The thresholds proposed here claim no more than that.

The corpus was assembled in two ways that are not equivalent, and the review describes itself
accordingly rather than as a single systematic search. The dual, glucagon containing and triple
agonist evidence comes from our own searching of three bibliographic databases and a trial registry.
The mono agonist comparator pool is adopted from the included study lists of two published reviews,
re screened against this review's criteria but not independently searched. The structure is closer to
an overview of reviews for the comparator arm with original searching for the agents those reviews do
not cover, and the flow diagram shows the two inflows separately for that reason.

## Note on the reference annotations

`docs/REFERENCES.md` annotates its entries with the role each plays in the argument, and two of those
annotations assert claims this review withdrew on 15 October after computing them. The annotation on
the retatrutide hepatic anchor states that liver fat is near identical at 8 and 12 mg while weight
loss continues to increase, and offers that as an argument against weight mediation; the computed
result is that both plateau, so the argument does not hold. The annotation on the GDF-15 entries
states that tirzepatide reduced GDF-15 while liraglutide did not; the computed result is that
dulaglutide reduced it by more than any tirzepatide dose and that no between group contrast reached
significance. Neither argument appears above. The reference file should be corrected, since its
stated purpose is to carry the arguments the Introduction and Discussion rest on, and in two places
it carries arguments that have been retired.
