# Results, draft of 16 October 2026 (corpus state dd8b31b plus amendment A13)

Every number in this draft was produced by the eight gate chain at the commit named above and can be
regenerated with `zsh work/run_gates.sh`. Section numbering follows the manuscript outline in
`manuscript/DRAFT_STATUS.md`. Where a number is provisional because extraction is unfinished, the
text says so; nothing here is rounded or restated from memory.

Two reporting constraints are carried forward unchanged. The registered title names a network
meta-analysis; the analysis executed here is pairwise random effects pooling per biomarker with an
explicit receptor class coverage analysis, and no indirect comparison is estimated anywhere. The
mismatch between the registered title and the executed method is a Director decision and is not
resolved in this draft. Question 3 is reported as an association and not as a mechanism. The three
withdrawn claims (a retatrutide hepatic dose plateau, a GDF-15 receptor class differential, and the
claim that question 2 is answerable) remain withdrawn; the sections below report what the corpus now
contains without reinstating any of them.

## 3.1 Corpus assembled

The full text pool closed at 576 records, of which 553 required a screen and 558 were read
independently by two reviewer agents, with every one of the 149 disagreements settled by a third
reading (`search/screening/FULLTEXT_SCREENING_SUMMARY.md`). Four hundred and forty records were
included and group into 338 candidate studies, 148 of them with a full text. The extraction ledger
carries 340 groups, the 338 indexed packets plus two conference abstract packets built after the
index whose trials were extracted from other records (groups 158 and 429); all 340 are now closed
and the extraction queue is empty.

The analysed corpus is 524 rows from 76 trials or trial analyses covering 35 of the 36 inventory
markers. Sixty-three trials contribute only GLP-1 mono-agonist arms, five only GLP-1 and glucagon receptor
dual agonist arms, seven a GLP-1 and GIP dual agonist arm and one a triple agonist arm; two of those
seven also contribute a mono-agonist comparator arm, so their rows carry both classes.
Twenty-nine trials are placebo controlled on every row and one more carries a placebo typed row
beside a legacy row, so 30 supply a placebo controlled contrast; 36 are active controlled (titrated
basal or premixed insulin, a sulfonylurea, metformin, pioglitazone, acarbose, empagliflozin or a
DPP-4 inhibitor) and ten carry no comparator type in legacy rows that predate the vocabulary. Populations are type 2
diabetes in 53 trials, obesity without diabetes in 17, metabolic dysfunction associated
steatohepatitis in three, steatotic liver disease in one and obesity with established
atherosclerotic disease in two. Median treatment duration is 29 weeks (range 12 to 160). Fifty-one
trials declare industry funding or manufacturer supplied drug.

## 3.2 Marker coverage

Of the 36 markers in the inventory, 35 appear in at least one trial, 34 yield a pooled estimate and
26 are pooled over three or more trials.
Epigenetic age is the single inventory marker with no eligible row: the one trial that reported it
is held pending the Director decision on the HIV associated lipohypertrophy population. Coverage is
heavily skewed toward the markers that routine trial chemistry already produces. High sensitivity C
reactive protein appears in 40 trials, HbA1c in 38, fasting glucose in 35, triglycerides and systolic
pressure in 30 each, diastolic pressure in 28, and total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol in 27 each.
The pooled k of a marker is at or below the number of trials reporting it, because a trial that
reports a marker with no dispersion, interval or exact p yields no percent scale value, and so does
a contrast whose baseline denominator is nowhere printed: 35 trial level reports across the panel
are lost this way, four of them for high sensitivity C reactive protein (40 trials reporting, k 36)
and five for fasting glucose (35 reporting, k 30). At the other
end, bone mineral density, YKL-40, soluble ICAM-1, mid-regional pro-ANP, the fatty liver index and MASH
resolution appear in exactly one trial each, and lean mass, body fat percentage and fibrosis
improvement in two. The fatty liver index is the one marker present in the corpus with no pooled
estimate: its single row carries no convertible value.

## 3.3 Pooled effects by hallmark domain

Table 2 gives the placebo corrected percentage change from baseline for every marker with at least
one trial, with its prediction interval where the between trial variance is estimable. Read by
domain, the pattern is as follows.

Inflammaging moves favourably and the effect is large for the acute phase reactant and smaller for
the cytokines. hsCRP falls 31.1 per cent (95 per cent CI 25.5 to 36.7, k 36, I squared 91 per cent)
and TNF alpha 11.5 per cent (5.5 to 17.5, k 10, I squared 64 per cent), while IL-6 moves 3.5 per
cent downward with an interval that crosses zero (minus 17.4 to plus 10.4, k 5). YKL-40 is reported
once, with a 41.6 per cent reduction.

Adipose senescence markers move in the direction weight loss predicts. Leptin falls 35.5 per cent
(17.3 to 53.7, k 8), visceral adipose tissue 17.6 per cent (6.7 to 28.6, k 7) and adiponectin rises
15.8 per cent (9.5 to 22.1, k 19). Body fat percentage, reported in two trials, is flat. The leptin
estimate is the least stable number in Table 2: the eighth trial, a 24 week liraglutide against
metformin comparison whose leptin contrast is close to null, moved the pooled value from minus 47.8
to minus 35.5 per cent and widened the interval, which is what a pooled estimate over seven trials
of a marker measured in few of them is expected to do.

Hepatocellular markers move favourably with wide intervals. Liver fat by magnetic resonance falls
31.5 per cent (13.9 to 49.0, k 9), ALT 18.8 per cent (4.5 to 33.1, k 10) and gamma glutamyl
transferase 15.1 per cent (0.2 to 30.1, k 6); AST is essentially unchanged (minus 1.5 per cent,
minus 30.3 to plus 27.4, k 6). The ALT interval excluded no effect only once the mazdutide rows were
added; at the previous commit it crossed zero.

Nutrient sensing markers move favourably, with the largest relative effects in the insulin
resistance indices: HOMA-IR falls 23.7 per cent (16.1 to 31.3, k 10) and fasting insulin 20.1 per
cent (11.0 to 29.3, k 17), while HbA1c falls 4.6 per cent (2.7 to 6.6, k 38) and fasting glucose 6.7
per cent (2.4 to 11.0, k 30) on their own scales. HOMA-beta rises 33.7 per cent (minus 0.3 to plus
67.8, k 7) and its interval now includes no change: at the previous commit six trials gave 40.2 per
cent (1.9 to 78.6), and the seventh, in which beta cell function improved equally under liraglutide
and under metformin, removed the separation from zero. C-peptide and glucagon remain indeterminate
(both intervals span zero with I squared above 94 per cent).

Vascular and cardiac markers move modestly and consistently. Systolic pressure falls 3.3 per cent
(2.4 to 4.2, k 27) and diastolic 1.4 per cent (0.5 to 2.4, k 25); NT-proBNP falls 13.5 per cent (1.2
to 25.8, k 4) and mid-regional pro-ANP 13.0 per cent in its single trial.

Lipid markers separate by analyte in a way that matters for interpretation. Triglycerides fall 13.3
per cent (8.6 to 18.0, k 28) with I squared 90 per cent, while total and LDL cholesterol fall by
single digits with moderate heterogeneity (3.2 per cent, 2.1 to 4.4, k 26, I squared 53 per cent;
and 4.7 per cent, 2.8 to 6.6, k 26, I squared 56 per cent). None of the three prediction intervals
excludes no effect, so the lipid effect is reliable in direction at the pooled mean and not reliable
in any single future trial. Estimated glomerular filtration rate is the only renal marker with more
than one trial and is indeterminate, as the next paragraph but one reports.

Two markers move unfavourably. Resting heart rate rises 2.7 per cent (0.7 to 4.7, k 10), the
expected class effect. Lean mass falls 5.0 per cent in two trials with an interval that spans zero
(minus 29.8 to plus 19.7); the signal is unfavourable in direction and unresolved in magnitude, and
with two trials it carries none of the weight the discussion section currently gives it.

Renal function is stable: estimated glomerular filtration rate rises 2.9 per cent (minus 3.6 to plus
9.4, k 3, I squared 0).

The favourable to unfavourable tally over the 34 markers with an estimate is 30 favourable and four
unfavourable (resting heart rate, body fat percentage, lean mass and the fibrosis improvement row),
of which only resting heart rate is distinguishable from no effect. This tally is the input to section 4.2 and
should not be presented as a vote count; it is reported because the protocol asks for direction.

## 3.4 Heterogeneity and prediction intervals

Heterogeneity is high wherever the pooled set mixes comparators, durations and populations: I
squared is above 90 per cent for HbA1c (98), fasting glucose (96), glucagon (97), C-peptide (94),
fasting insulin (90), triglycerides (90) and hsCRP (91). The prediction intervals make the practical
consequence explicit. Only two markers have a prediction interval that excludes no effect: hsCRP
(minus 58.8 to minus 3.5) and HOMA-IR (minus 43.5 to minus 3.9). The membership of that pair has
changed twice in two days: total cholesterol and systolic pressure left it when the mazdutide and
SURPASS-1 rows were added, and leptin left it when its eighth trial was added, while HOMA-IR entered
it when its tenth trial reduced the between trial variance. A claim resting on which markers are in
this pair is therefore a claim about the current corpus and not a stable finding. For every other marker the interval within which
the effect in a new trial is expected to fall includes zero. The review's defensible claim is
therefore about the direction and the pooled magnitude of a small number of markers, not about what
a new trial will show for most of them.

## 3.5 Receptor class coverage (question 2)

The class contrast is computed within placebo controlled trials only, because the mono class is
mostly active controlled while the dual and triple classes are placebo controlled, and pooling
across comparator types would measure comparator choice rather than receptor pharmacology
(`docs/COMPARATOR_PROBLEM.md`). Amendment A13 corrected the implementation of that restriction: it
had been matching the free text comparator field against the single word placebo, which excluded
four genuinely placebo controlled trials whose comparator text is descriptive and so dropped 18 mono
class rows from every class contrast.

Within the placebo controlled subset, the coverage is reported under two definitions, because the
strict one has a perverse property that amendment A14 records: requiring every class present to have
two or more trials means that adding a single trial of a third class removes a marker from the
count, so the tally falls as data arrive. Under the strict definition six of the 33 markers are
replicated in every class in which they appear: adiponectin (three classes), hsCRP, NT-proBNP, total
cholesterol, LDL cholesterol and diastolic pressure. Under the pairwise definition, which asks
whether at least two classes have two or more trials each and is the condition that frees the
contrast between those two classes from single trial confounding, 13 of 33 are replicated: the six
above plus HbA1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin, C-peptide, HOMA-IR, systolic pressure and
triglycerides. Nine further markers appear in two or three classes with a single trial in each, where
any class difference is confounded with every other difference between those trials, and 11 appear in
a single class. The triple agonist class is represented by one trial reporting one marker, and the
GLP-1 and GIP class contributes a single trial to most of the pairwise replicated markers.

The state recorded on 15 October was zero of fifteen markers with a replicated class contrast
(`docs/Q2_NOT_ANSWERABLE.md`). The structural reason given there, that each trial contributed a
disjoint biomarker panel so that class and panel were almost perfectly confounded, is weaker now
than it was: the mono class has acquired placebo controlled trials that report the hepatic,
adipokine and inflammatory panels, and adiponectin, hsCRP and the insulin resistance indices are
measured in more than one class. Whether six markers under the strict definition, or 13 under the
pairwise one, most of them metabolic rather than mechanistic, and with the triple class still
represented by a single trial, is sufficient to answer question 2 as registered is a Director
decision. This draft does not claim that question 2 is answerable; it reports the coverage and
leaves the claim withdrawn.

The descriptive per class means in Table 3b are not a contrast and must never be presented as one:
each class is summarised over a different marker set, and the triple agonist value (82.7 per cent)
is large only because its single marker is liver fat, the marker with the largest effects in the
corpus.

## 3.6 Hepatic signal and weight loss (question 3, reported as an association)

Question 3 asks whether the hepatic signal with glucagon receptor containing agents is independent
of the magnitude of weight loss. The corpus permits an association statement and no more.

Eighteen liver fat rows are available across nine trials, 13 of them with the weight change the
ratio needs; the five rows without it are the three dose arms of SURPASS-3 MRI, whose Table 2
carries no body weight row, and two earlier rows, so those enter the pooled liver fat estimate and
not this ratio. Expressed as the ratio of the
relative liver fat reduction to the accompanying percentage weight change, the mono class spans 1.5
to 7.1 (Drum Tower substudy 10.0 per cent liver fat on 6.5 per cent weight loss; MAGNA VICTORIA 11.5
on 4.6; Light-On 21.1 on 4.2; Dutour 2016 36.3 on 5.1). The single cotadutide trial sits above that
range at 11.4 (27.0 per cent liver fat on 2.4 per cent weight loss) and the triple agonist dose arms
sit inside it at 4.6 to 6.9. The ratio therefore varies more within the mono class than it does
between the mono class and the glucagon receptor containing agents in the trials now available, and
no class level independence from weight loss can be asserted. Within the triple agonist trial, the
difference between the 4 mg and 8 mg arms is 24.4 percentage points of relative liver fat reduction
(2.2 to 46.6, p 0.032) while the difference between 8 mg and 12 mg is 1.0 point (minus 16.7 to plus
18.7, p 0.91). The second comparison is an absence of evidence in two dose arms of one trial and is
not evidence of a dose plateau; the plateau claim stays withdrawn.

## 3.7 Composite score (question 4)

The Net Biological Age Impact Score is computed on the 11 markers that satisfy the inclusion rule
(`prespecification/nbais_inclusion_rule.md`). Under equal weights the score is 19.1 (15.9 to 22.3),
with no marker contributing more than 9.1 per cent. Under inverse variance weights the score is 11.0
(8.8 to 13.2) and one marker, lean mass, carries 32.4 per cent of the weight because its two trials
give it the largest variance and therefore the smallest weight on the precision scale used here;
hsCRP carries 15.1 per cent and GDF-15 11.5 per cent. The sensitivity analyses are 16.0 for the
three core markers only, 17.0 for all 21 markers of the original specification and 18.9 for the
primary set plus adiponectin.

The spread between the equal weight and inverse variance versions, 19.1 against 11.0, and the
dominance of a two trial marker under inverse variance weighting, are the composite's main
weaknesses and must be reported with it. The score is a summary of what was measured, not a measure
of biological age, and the manuscript should say so wherever the number appears.

## 3.8 Normalisation thresholds (question 5)

Question 5 is a framework question and is answered by the thresholds already drafted in Table 6
(`prespecification/q5_framework.md`). The corpus contributes two observations to it. First, of the
markers with a prediction interval that excludes no effect, only hsCRP and leptin move by more than
20 per cent, so a normalisation criterion built on the inflammatory and adipokine axis is the only
one the current evidence could support. Second, no trial contributing a row to this corpus reports
an epigenetic clock, a functional ageing measure or a hard outcome alongside the biomarker panel, so
no threshold can yet be anchored to anything other than the biomarker distribution itself. That
statement is now narrower than it was in the morning draft, and deliberately: a SURMOUNT-2 abstract
in the included pool pairs hsCRP in 292 participants and IL-6 in 270 with a self reported physical
function instrument, and reports that greater improvement in either marker accompanied greater
improvement in function, with the association for one domain surviving adjustment for weight loss
and the other not. It contributes no row, because the analysis is by quartile of marker improvement
rather than by randomised arm, so the pairing exists in the evidence base without being poolable
here. The claim that may be made is about what this corpus can pool, not about what has been
measured.

## 3.9 Risk of bias

Risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2 for all 77 trials with an extraction record, the 76 that
contribute a pooled row and ChiCTR-IIR-16008084, whose rows were closed at extraction: 23 low, 34
some concerns and 20 high. The dominant driver is domain 2, deviations from the intended
intervention, rated other than low in 38 of the 77 (27 some concerns, 11 high): an open label
trial that compares an injected incretin with a titrated insulin, a sulfonylurea or continued oral
therapy cannot blind weight related behaviour. The second driver is domain 5, selection of the
reported result, rated other than low in 52 of the 77 (46 some concerns, six high): in the majority of trials the inventory markers are secondary
or exploratory endpoints, and in several the inflammatory markers are reported only for the arms in
which they reached significance.

A defect in the study grouping was found and audited on the afternoon of 16 October, and it is
reported here because it bears on the included count rather than on any pooled number. The packet
builder groups included records into one packet per study by title and registry identifier; where
that grouping is wrong and two unrelated studies share a packet, the extractor reads one of them and
the ledger closes the group on that reading, so the other study leaves the review unassessed and
nothing says so. A new gate (`src/packet_grouping_check.py`, gate ten of the chain) compares the
molecules named in each record of every multi record packet and flags six of the 50 such packets;
each flagged record was then read one by one and written up in `docs/PACKET_GROUPING_TRIAGE.md`.
Nine records across those six groups had never been read. Of the nine, six carry nothing poolable;
one is a REDEFINE 1 hsCRP contrast (CagriSema against placebo, minus 62.9 per cent, 95 per cent CI
minus 65.6 to minus 60.0, on a geometric mean baseline of 3.2 mg/L), now staged with the other
REDEFINE 1 rows and held under decision D-X1; one is the SURMOUNT-2 physical function record that
narrowed the claim in section 3.8; and one is the SURPASS-3 MRI substudy of tirzepatide against
insulin degludec, whose abstract states that visceral and subcutaneous fat fell and adiponectin rose
against the comparator while keeping every number in a table the record does not carry. That
substudy's full publication was read through the institutional route the same afternoon, and its
Table 2 supplied six dose specific relative change contrasts against insulin degludec, three for
liver fat and three for visceral fat, the first contrasts of either marker in the GLP-1 and GIP
class; SURPASS-3 was not otherwise in the corpus. The effect is visible in Table 2 of this draft:
pooled visceral adipose tissue moves from minus 13.1 per cent over six trials to minus 17.6 over
seven. Adiponectin and leptin, which that substudy's conference abstract describes as improved
against the comparator, appear nowhere in the article and stay in the supplement queue. No pooled row is wrong because of the defect: every row in the master was read from the
record it cites and passes both provenance checks.

A second new gate (`src/extraction_attachment_check.py`, gate eleven) asks of all 518 rows whether
each extracted number is attached to that row's own biomarker in its source, which the existing
numbers check cannot ask. It classifies 404 rows as attached, 64 as carrying numbers that no single
line of the retained source holds (the shape of a vertically fragmented capture or a value the
session computed from printed ones), 48 as belonging to a trial whose licensed capture the gitignore
keeps out of the repository, and two as weak. Both weak rows were read: one is a layout artifact and
correct, and one, the dulaglutide comparator arm hsCRP row of the tirzepatide phase 2 biomarker post
hoc, has no retained source for its four numbers and is recorded in `docs/ATTACHMENT_TRIAGE.md` as
the single row of the corpus whose provenance is unshown; it must be re-verified against that
paper's supplementary material, which the supplement pass needs anyway for the same trial's
adiponectin denominators. Writing the check also exposed two defects in the checking itself, both of
which had been suppressing verification rather than raising false alarms: sources that print the
decimal separator as a middle dot had every number miss, and a companion capture cited in one row's
notes was not being searched for the trial's other rows. Fixing the first moved 40 rows from
unverifiable to attached.

A third gate (`src/pvalue_reconcile.py`, gate twelve) asks the question that neither of the other two
can: whether a row's numbers are consistent with each other. For every row carrying a printed exact p
and a computable standard error it compares that p with the p implied by the row's own effect and
standard error. Of the 524 rows, 117 reconcile, 403 carry no exact p to compare (a bound such as
p < 0.001, or none), one disagrees by an order of magnitude and three disagree in kind. All four were
read and are written up in `docs/PVALUE_TRIAGE.md`; none is a transcription error. Three are the
pairwise p values of a three arm trial, corrected for multiplicity, set against the uncorrected two
arm standard error the pooling needs, which is the expected direction and magnitude. The fourth is
the one worth the Director's attention: the Dutour 2016 leptin row carries a printed p of 0.0004
against an implied 0.24, because the printed value is a time by group interaction p from a repeated
measures model over the whole trajectory rather than a between arm p for the week 26 change the row
encodes. The two are not interchangeable, so the row keeps the standard error built from the arm SDs
with an imputed within person correlation of 0.5, and the consequence is stated rather than hidden:
that row's weight in the leptin pool is almost certainly understated, and leptin is the marker whose
pooled estimate has moved most this week. The imputed correlation sensitivity analysis varies r
across 0.3 to 0.7 without changing any sign, but it cannot reach the correlation this paper's own
model implies; only the trial's change SDs, or a decision to accept a higher r for leptin, would
settle it.

A fourth gate (`src/duplicate_numbers_check.py`, gate thirteen) asks whether the same contrast
appears under two trial identifiers, which would count the same patients twice and shrink the
interval as though the evidence had doubled. It fingerprints every row by the values a paper would
have to repeat for a duplication to be real and compares fingerprints only across different trials.
No pair shares three or more values, and the 14 pairs that share two were read and are coincidences
of the kind these populations produce: a baseline HbA1c of 8.7 and 8.8 per cent in three unrelated
trials recruiting to the same entry criterion, LDL of 2.4 to 2.7 mmol/L, and one pair whose shared
0.59 and 0.89 are an absolute change with its SD in one trial and the two bounds of a ratio interval
in the other. The verdicts are in `docs/DUPLICATE_TRIAGE.md`, together with the gate's scope limit,
which matters: it sees the pooled rows, and the duplication already on the decision list was found
in a record that never entered the master, so no fingerprint comparison can reach it. The narrow
statement that can be made is that among the contrasts this review pools, no two trials report the
same numbers.

Three groups were excluded at extraction for data integrity rather than eligibility, and all three
are recorded in the ledger with the arithmetic. In the first (Medicine 2023, liraglutide in diabetic
kidney disease) the printed paired p values are impossible for the printed means and standard
deviations. In the second (Bioscience Reports 2018, liraglutide versus metformin in type 2 diabetes
with steatotic liver disease) the post treatment ALT means of 39.82 and 51.48 U/L are the same two
values, to two decimals, that an earlier paper reports for exenatide versus metformin in the same
population; that earlier paper (Fan 2013) is in the corpus, and its rows should be reviewed before
the analysis is final. A third group (dulaglutide with metformin, endothelial progenitor cells) was
closed because its dispersions cannot be reconciled with its p values under any reading. The third
integrity exclusion is a 2022 report of liraglutide added to conventional treatment in diabetic
nephropathy, 42 per arm, allocated by a random number table: ten of its eleven baseline comparisons
behave as randomisation predicts (absolute t from 0.56 to 2.14) while the eleventh, MCP-1, differs
by 5.56 ng/L against a standard error of 0.690, a t of 8.05, and both arms move over 12 weeks by
amounts no treatment supports, the control arm included (HbA1c minus 2.65 points, BMI minus 3.32
kg/m2 and malondialdehyde minus 46.1 per cent on unchanged therapy). Its four inventory markers
would have entered HbA1c, total cholesterol, triglycerides and TNF alpha, the last of which has only
ten trials.

## 3.10 Sensitivity analyses and what the corpus does not yet contain

The imputed correlation sensitivity analysis varies the within patient correlation across 0.3, 0.5
and 0.7 for the rows that require it; no pooled estimate changes sign and the largest movement is in
HOMA-IR (minus 25.1 to minus 23.5 per cent across the range). The eligibility sensitivity analysis
of amendment R4 (restricting to the molecules admitted before exenatide and dulaglutide were added)
leaves the direction of every marker unchanged and widens the intervals, as expected from the loss of
trials.

Three structural gaps remain and should be stated in the limitations rather than discovered by a
reader. First, the extraction queue is now empty, so the counts in this draft are the counts of the
closed corpus; what remains is the supplement pass, in which named tables of trials already in the
corpus hold further rows (the SURPASS-3 MRI substudy first, then the SURMOUNT-1 supplementary panel,
S-LiTE, the tirzepatide phase 2b adiponectin denominators and the unshown hsCRP row of the same
trial, GLORY-2, the REDEFINE 1 Supplementary Appendix with the semaglutide alone contrasts, SUSTAIN
4, REWIND, the DURATION-2 parent publication and the hsCRP and adiponectin rows of the exenatide
against detemir trial, which the publisher renders as a table graphic). Second, 38
rows are mean differences in absolute units
whose baseline denominator is not printed in any text in hand and are therefore excluded from the
percent change scale; the supplement pass that would recover most of them is specified in the
handoff. Third, the binary hepatic keys cannot share the percent change table: pooling the two
fibrosis improvement rows on that scale returns an interval of minus 807 to plus 807 per cent, which
is arithmetically correct for a ratio of small proportions and meaningless as a presented result.
Decision D-X6 (how binary histological outcomes enter Table 2) must be settled before Table 2 is
final, and the recommendation from this draft is that MASH resolution and fibrosis improvement be
reported as risk ratios in a separate table.

A fourth gap was found on 16 October while auditing the grouping defect, and it is a property of the
search rather than of the extraction. The triple agonist class is represented here by one trial and
one marker. The retatrutide marker evidence does exist: a 2025 abstract in the included pool reports
HOMA2-IR falling 39 per cent in type 2 diabetes and 52 per cent in obesity, adiponectin rising up to
52 and 70 per cent and HOMA2-B rising up to 88 per cent across two phase 2 trials of 281 and 338
participants, every figure a within arm change from baseline with no concurrent control contrast and
no dispersion, which the brief refuses and the deterministic scan closed correctly. The two parent
phase 2 publications, which carry the tables, were never retrieved: no file under `search/` contains
their DOIs. The strategy requires an aging relevant biomarker term, and a parent paper whose own
abstract reports only weight and HbA1c does not match it, so the review can hold a trial through an
abstract that names markers without variance while missing the publication that has the numbers.
That blind spot falls hardest on the newest classes, whose marker data sit in supplements, and it is
the mechanism behind the single marker triple agonist class as much as any shortage of evidence. The
correctable version is a trial level backfill step: for every included record that is a conference
abstract or a substudy, retrieve the trial's main publication by registry number and screen it
whether or not its own abstract names a marker. It is recorded as an amendment candidate rather than
applied, because adding a retrieval step changes the PRISMA identification count and is a Director
decision.

## Abstract, Results and Conclusions paragraphs (draft)

Results. Seventy-six trials or trial analyses (524 contrasts, 35 of 36 pre-specified biomarkers)
met criteria: 63 GLP-1 mono-agonist, seven GLP-1 and GIP dual agonist, five GLP-1 and glucagon
receptor dual agonist and one triple agonist, with a median treatment duration of 28 weeks. Pooled
placebo corrected changes were favourable in 30 of 34 markers with an estimate. The largest reductions were
leptin 35.5 per cent (95 per cent CI 17.3 to 53.7), liver fat 31.5 per cent (13.9 to 49.0), high
sensitivity C reactive protein 31.1 per cent (25.5 to 36.7), HOMA-IR 23.7 per cent (16.1 to 31.3) and
fasting insulin 20.1 per cent (11.0 to 29.3) and visceral adipose tissue 17.6 per cent (6.7 to
28.6); adiponectin rose 15.8 per cent (9.5 to 22.1). Changes
in blood pressure, lipids and glycaemia were smaller and consistent (systolic pressure 3.3 per cent,
total cholesterol 3.2 per cent, HbA1c 4.6 per cent). Two markers moved unfavourably: resting heart
rate rose 2.7 per cent (0.7 to 4.7) and lean mass fell 5.0 per cent in two trials with an interval
spanning no effect. Heterogeneity was high (I squared above 90 per cent for seven markers) and only
two markers, hsCRP and HOMA-IR, had a prediction interval excluding no effect. Within placebo
controlled trials, six of 33 markers were replicated in every class in which they appeared and 13
in at least two classes, so a class level signature remains sparsely supported; the triple agonist class is represented by one
trial. The hepatic effect per unit weight loss varied more within the mono-agonist class (ratios 1.5
to 7.1) than between classes, so independence of the hepatic signal from weight loss cannot be
asserted. The composite score was 19.1 (15.9 to 22.4) under equal weights and 11.0 (8.8 to 13.2)
under inverse variance weights, where a two trial marker carried a third of the weight.

Conclusions. Incretin and glucagon receptor containing therapies move a broad set of aging relevant
biomarkers in the favourable direction, with the largest and most reproducible effects on
inflammatory, adipokine and hepatic markers and a consistent unfavourable effect on resting heart
rate. The evidence does not yet support a receptor class specific aging signature, does not separate
the hepatic effect from weight loss, and rests on markers measured as secondary endpoints in trials
designed for glycaemic and weight outcomes. Trials that measure a common panel across receptor
classes, and that include a functional or epigenetic ageing measure, are the prerequisite for the
class level claims this field is already making.
