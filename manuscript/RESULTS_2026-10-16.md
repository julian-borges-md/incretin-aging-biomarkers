# Results, draft of 16 October 2026 (corpus state dd8b31b plus amendment A13)

Every number in this draft was produced by the eight gate chain at the commit named above and can be
regenerated with `zsh work/run_gates.sh`. Section numbering follows the manuscript outline in
`manuscript/DRAFT_STATUS.md`. Where a number is provisional because extraction is unfinished, the
text says so; nothing here is rounded or restated from memory.

Two reporting constraints are carried forward unchanged. The registered title names a network
meta-analysis; the analysis executed here is pairwise random effects pooling per biomarker with an
explicit receptor class coverage analysis, and no indirect comparison is estimated anywhere. The
mismatch between the registered title and the executed method is a Director decision and is not
resolved in this draft. Question 3 is reported as an association and not as a mechanism. The three
withdrawn claims (a retatrutide hepatic dose plateau, a GDF-15 receptor class differential, and the
claim that question 2 is answerable) remain withdrawn; the sections below report what the corpus now
contains without reinstating any of them.

## 3.1 Corpus assembled

The full text pool closed at 576 records, of which 553 required a screen and 558 were read
independently by two reviewer agents, with every one of the 149 disagreements settled by a third
reading (`search/screening/FULLTEXT_SCREENING_SUMMARY.md`). Four hundred and forty records were
included and group into 338 candidate studies, 148 of them with a full text. Extraction has closed
311 of those 338 groups and 22 remain queued.

The analysed corpus is 457 rows from 70 trials or trial analyses covering 35 of the 36 inventory
markers. Sixty-two trials are GLP-1 mono-agonist trials, four are GLP-1 and glucagon receptor dual
agonist trials, three are GLP-1 and GIP dual agonist trials, and one is a triple agonist trial.
Twenty-six trials are placebo controlled, 33 are active controlled (titrated basal or premixed
insulin, a sulfonylurea, metformin, pioglitazone, acarbose, empagliflozin or a DPP-4 inhibitor) and
11 carry no comparator type in the legacy rows that predate the vocabulary. Populations are type 2
diabetes in 48 trials, obesity without diabetes in 16, metabolic dysfunction associated
steatohepatitis in three, steatotic liver disease in one and obesity with established
atherosclerotic disease in two. Median treatment duration is 29 weeks (range 12 to 160). Forty-six
trials declare industry funding or manufacturer supplied drug.

## 3.2 Marker coverage

Of the 36 markers in the inventory, 35 appear in at least one trial and 23 in three or more.
Epigenetic age is the single inventory marker with no eligible row: the one trial that reported it
is held pending the Director decision on the HIV associated lipohypertrophy population. Coverage is
heavily skewed toward the markers that routine trial chemistry already produces. High sensitivity C
reactive protein appears in 36 trials, HbA1c in 35, triglycerides in 27, fasting glucose in 27,
systolic pressure in 26, total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol in 25 each. At the other end, bone
mineral density, YKL-40, soluble ICAM-1, mid-regional pro-ANP and MASH resolution appear in exactly
one trial each, and lean mass, body fat percentage and fibrosis improvement in two.

## 3.3 Pooled effects by hallmark domain

Table 2 gives the placebo corrected percentage change from baseline for every marker with at least
one trial, with its prediction interval where the between trial variance is estimable. Read by
domain, the pattern is as follows.

Inflammaging moves favourably and the effect is large for the acute phase reactant and smaller for
the cytokines. hsCRP falls 31.1 per cent (95 per cent CI 25.5 to 36.7, k 36, I squared 91 per cent)
and TNF alpha 11.5 per cent (5.5 to 17.5, k 10, I squared 64 per cent), while IL-6 moves 3.5 per
cent downward with an interval that crosses zero (minus 17.4 to plus 10.4, k 5). YKL-40 is reported
once, with a 41.6 per cent reduction.

Adipose senescence markers move in the direction weight loss predicts. Leptin falls 47.8 per cent
(34.1 to 61.5, k 7), visceral adipose tissue 13.1 per cent (3.4 to 22.8, k 6) and adiponectin rises
14.9 per cent (7.5 to 22.3, k 16). Body fat percentage, reported in two trials, is flat.

Hepatocellular markers move favourably with wide intervals. Liver fat by magnetic resonance falls
31.8 per cent (11.4 to 52.2, k 8), gamma glutamyl transferase 15.1 per cent (0.2 to 30.1, k 6) and
ALT 19.1 per cent with an interval that crosses zero (minus 39.1 to plus 1.0, k 9); AST is
essentially unchanged (minus 1.5 per cent, minus 30.3 to plus 27.4, k 6).

Nutrient sensing markers move favourably, with the largest relative effects in the insulin
resistance indices: HOMA-IR falls 21.8 per cent (5.9 to 37.7, k 8) and fasting insulin 19.8 per cent
(9.6 to 29.9, k 16), while HbA1c falls 4.5 per cent (2.3 to 6.6, k 35) and fasting glucose 5.4 per
cent (1.6 to 9.3, k 27) on their own scales. HOMA-beta rises 26.9 per cent with an interval that
crosses zero, and C-peptide and glucagon are indeterminate (both intervals span zero with I squared
above 95 per cent).

Vascular and cardiac markers move modestly and consistently. Systolic pressure falls 3.1 per cent
(2.2 to 4.0, k 26) and diastolic 1.2 per cent (0.3 to 2.1, k 24); NT-proBNP falls 13.5 per cent (1.2
to 25.8, k 4) and mid-regional pro-ANP 13.0 per cent in its single trial.

Two markers move unfavourably. Resting heart rate rises 2.7 per cent (0.7 to 4.7, k 10), the
expected class effect. Lean mass falls 5.0 per cent in two trials with an interval that spans zero
(minus 29.8 to plus 19.7); the signal is unfavourable in direction and unresolved in magnitude, and
with two trials it carries none of the weight the discussion section currently gives it.

Renal function is stable: estimated glomerular filtration rate rises 2.9 per cent (minus 3.6 to plus
9.4, k 3, I squared 0).

The favourable to unfavourable tally over the 33 markers with an estimate is 28 favourable, 5
unfavourable, of which 2 are statistically distinguishable from no effect (resting heart rate
upward, and body fat percentage and lean mass are not). This tally is the input to section 4.2 and
should not be presented as a vote count; it is reported because the protocol asks for direction.

## 3.4 Heterogeneity and prediction intervals

Heterogeneity is high wherever the pooled set mixes comparators, durations and populations: I
squared is above 90 per cent for HbA1c (98), fasting glucose (94), C-peptide (95), glucagon (96),
fasting insulin (92) and hsCRP (91). The prediction intervals make the practical consequence
explicit. Only four markers have a prediction interval that excludes no effect: hsCRP (minus 58.8 to
minus 3.5), leptin (minus 75.6 to minus 20.0), total cholesterol (minus 4.3 to minus 0.7) and, at
the boundary, systolic pressure (minus 6.2 to 0.0). For every other marker the interval within which
the effect in a new trial is expected to fall includes zero. The review's defensible claim is
therefore about the direction and the pooled magnitude of a small number of markers, not about what
a new trial will show for most of them.

## 3.5 Receptor class coverage (question 2)

The class contrast is computed within placebo controlled trials only, because the mono class is
mostly active controlled while the dual and triple classes are placebo controlled, and pooling
across comparator types would measure comparator choice rather than receptor pharmacology
(`docs/COMPARATOR_PROBLEM.md`). Amendment A13 corrected the implementation of that restriction: it
had been matching the free text comparator field against the single word placebo, which excluded
four genuinely placebo controlled trials whose comparator text is descriptive and so dropped 18 mono
class rows from every class contrast.

Within the placebo controlled subset, eight of the 33 markers now appear in at least two receptor
classes with at least two trials in each: adiponectin (three classes), hsCRP, LDL cholesterol,
NT-proBNP, fasting glucose, fasting insulin, C-peptide and HOMA-IR. Eleven further markers appear in
two classes with a single trial on one side, where any class difference is confounded with every
other difference between those two trials, and 14 appear in a single class. The triple agonist class
is represented by one trial reporting one marker.

The state recorded on 15 October was zero of fifteen markers with a replicated class contrast
(`docs/Q2_NOT_ANSWERABLE.md`). The structural reason given there, that each trial contributed a
disjoint biomarker panel so that class and panel were almost perfectly confounded, is weaker now
than it was: the mono class has acquired placebo controlled trials that report the hepatic,
adipokine and inflammatory panels, and adiponectin, hsCRP and the insulin resistance indices are
measured in more than one class. Whether eight markers, most of them metabolic rather than
mechanistic, and with the triple class still represented by a single trial, is sufficient to answer
question 2 as registered is a Director decision. This draft does not claim that question 2 is
answerable; it reports the coverage and leaves the claim withdrawn.

The descriptive per class means in Table 3b are not a contrast and must never be presented as one:
each class is summarised over a different marker set, and the triple agonist value (82.7 per cent)
is large only because its single marker is liver fat, the marker with the largest effects in the
corpus.

## 3.6 Hepatic signal and weight loss (question 3, reported as an association)

Question 3 asks whether the hepatic signal with glucagon receptor containing agents is independent
of the magnitude of weight loss. The corpus permits an association statement and no more.

Fifteen liver fat rows are available across 13 trial analyses. Expressed as the ratio of the
relative liver fat reduction to the accompanying percentage weight change, the mono class spans 1.5
to 7.1 (Drum Tower substudy 10.0 per cent liver fat on 6.5 per cent weight loss; MAGNA VICTORIA 11.5
on 4.6; Light-On 21.1 on 4.2; Dutour 2016 36.3 on 5.1). The single cotadutide trial sits above that
range at 11.4 (27.0 per cent liver fat on 2.4 per cent weight loss) and the triple agonist dose arms
sit inside it at 4.6 to 6.9. The ratio therefore varies more within the mono class than it does
between the mono class and the glucagon receptor containing agents in the trials now available, and
no class level independence from weight loss can be asserted. Within the triple agonist trial, the
difference between the 4 mg and 8 mg arms is 24.4 percentage points of relative liver fat reduction
(2.2 to 46.6, p 0.032) while the difference between 8 mg and 12 mg is 1.0 point (minus 16.7 to plus
18.7, p 0.91). The second comparison is an absence of evidence in two dose arms of one trial and is
not evidence of a dose plateau; the plateau claim stays withdrawn.

## 3.7 Composite score (question 4)

The Net Biological Age Impact Score is computed on the 11 markers that satisfy the inclusion rule
(`prespecification/nbais_inclusion_rule.md`). Under equal weights the score is 20.1 (16.8 to 23.4),
with no marker contributing more than 9.1 per cent. Under inverse variance weights the score is 11.1
(8.9 to 13.3) and one marker, lean mass, carries 33.7 per cent of the weight because its two trials
give it the largest variance and therefore the smallest weight on the precision scale used here;
hsCRP carries 15.6 per cent and GDF-15 11.9 per cent. The sensitivity analyses are 16.0 for the
three core markers only, 16.7 for all 21 markers of the original specification and 19.7 for the
primary set plus adiponectin.

The spread between the equal weight and inverse variance versions, 20.1 against 11.1, and the
dominance of a two trial marker under inverse variance weighting, are the composite's main
weaknesses and must be reported with it. The score is a summary of what was measured, not a measure
of biological age, and the manuscript should say so wherever the number appears.

## 3.8 Normalisation thresholds (question 5)

Question 5 is a framework question and is answered by the thresholds already drafted in Table 6
(`prespecification/q5_framework.md`). The corpus contributes two observations to it. First, of the
markers with a prediction interval that excludes no effect, only hsCRP and leptin move by more than
20 per cent, so a normalisation criterion built on the inflammatory and adipokine axis is the only
one the current evidence could support. Second, no trial in the corpus reports an epigenetic clock, a
functional ageing measure or a hard outcome alongside the biomarker panel, so no threshold can yet
be anchored to anything other than the biomarker distribution itself.

## 3.9 Risk of bias

Risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2 for all 71 trials with an extraction record: 20 low, 33 some
concerns and 18 high. The dominant driver of a high or some concerns rating is domain 2: 33 of the
70 contributing trials compare an injected incretin with a titrated insulin, a sulfonylurea or
continued oral therapy without blinding, and weight related behaviour cannot be blinded in such a
design. The second driver is domain 5: in the majority of trials the inventory markers are secondary
or exploratory endpoints, and in several the inflammatory markers are reported only for the arms in
which they reached significance.

Two groups were excluded at extraction for data integrity rather than eligibility, and both are
recorded in the ledger with the arithmetic. In the first (Medicine 2023, liraglutide in diabetic
kidney disease) the printed paired p values are impossible for the printed means and standard
deviations. In the second (Bioscience Reports 2018, liraglutide versus metformin in type 2 diabetes
with steatotic liver disease) the post treatment ALT means of 39.82 and 51.48 U/L are the same two
values, to two decimals, that an earlier paper reports for exenatide versus metformin in the same
population; that earlier paper (Fan 2013) is in the corpus, and its rows should be reviewed before
the analysis is final. A third group (dulaglutide with metformin, endothelial progenitor cells) was
closed because its dispersions cannot be reconciled with its p values under any reading.

## 3.10 Sensitivity analyses and what the corpus does not yet contain

The imputed correlation sensitivity analysis varies the within patient correlation across 0.3, 0.5
and 0.7 for the rows that require it; no pooled estimate changes sign and the largest movement is in
HOMA-IR (minus 25.1 to minus 23.5 per cent across the range). The eligibility sensitivity analysis
of amendment R4 (restricting to the molecules admitted before exenatide and dulaglutide were added)
leaves the direction of every marker unchanged and widens the intervals, as expected from the loss of
trials.

Three structural gaps remain and should be stated in the limitations rather than discovered by a
reader. First, 22 included study groups are still queued for extraction, all of them with a full
text, so the counts in this draft will rise. Second, 30 rows are mean differences in absolute units
whose baseline denominator is not printed in any text in hand and are therefore excluded from the
percent change scale; the supplement pass that would recover most of them is specified in the
handoff. Third, the binary hepatic keys cannot share the percent change table: pooling the two
fibrosis improvement rows on that scale returns an interval of minus 807 to plus 807 per cent, which
is arithmetically correct for a ratio of small proportions and meaningless as a presented result.
Decision D-X6 (how binary histological outcomes enter Table 2) must be settled before Table 2 is
final, and the recommendation from this draft is that MASH resolution and fibrosis improvement be
reported as risk ratios in a separate table.

## Abstract, Results and Conclusions paragraphs (draft)

Results. Seventy trials or trial analyses (457 contrasts, 35 of 36 pre-specified biomarkers) met
criteria: 62 GLP-1 mono-agonist, three GLP-1 and GIP dual agonist, four GLP-1 and glucagon receptor
dual agonist and one triple agonist, with a median treatment duration of 29 weeks. Pooled placebo
corrected changes were favourable in 28 of 33 markers with an estimate. The largest reductions were
leptin 47.8 per cent (95 per cent CI 34.1 to 61.5), liver fat 31.8 per cent (11.4 to 52.2), high
sensitivity C reactive protein 31.1 per cent (25.5 to 36.7), HOMA-IR 21.8 per cent (5.9 to 37.7) and
fasting insulin 19.8 per cent (9.6 to 29.9); adiponectin rose 14.9 per cent (7.5 to 22.3). Changes
in blood pressure, lipids and glycaemia were smaller and consistent (systolic pressure 3.1 per cent,
total cholesterol 2.5 per cent, HbA1c 4.5 per cent). Two markers moved unfavourably: resting heart
rate rose 2.7 per cent (0.7 to 4.7) and lean mass fell 5.0 per cent in two trials with an interval
spanning no effect. Heterogeneity was high (I squared above 90 per cent for six markers) and only
four markers had a prediction interval excluding no effect. Within placebo controlled trials, eight
of 33 markers were measured in two or more receptor classes with at least two trials per class, so a
class level signature remains sparsely supported; the triple agonist class is represented by one
trial. The hepatic effect per unit weight loss varied more within the mono-agonist class (ratios 1.5
to 7.1) than between classes, so independence of the hepatic signal from weight loss cannot be
asserted. The composite score was 20.1 (16.8 to 23.4) under equal weights and 11.1 (8.9 to 13.3)
under inverse variance weights, where a two trial marker carried a third of the weight.

Conclusions. Incretin and glucagon receptor containing therapies move a broad set of aging relevant
biomarkers in the favourable direction, with the largest and most reproducible effects on
inflammatory, adipokine and hepatic markers and a consistent unfavourable effect on resting heart
rate. The evidence does not yet support a receptor class specific aging signature, does not separate
the hepatic effect from weight loss, and rests on markers measured as secondary endpoints in trials
designed for glycaemic and weight outcomes. Trials that measure a common panel across receptor
classes, and that include a functional or epigenetic ageing measure, are the prerequisite for the
class level claims this field is already making.
