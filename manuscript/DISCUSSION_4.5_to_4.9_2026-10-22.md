# Discussion, sections 4.5 to 4.9

Draft of 22 October 2026, written against head `3f51e38`. These five sections depend on none of the
fifteen decisions in `docs/DIRECTOR_DECISIONS_2026-10-19.md` and are drafted while that register is
open. Sections 4.1 to 4.4 and Section 5 depend on A1, A2, A3 and A4 and are not drafted here.

Three claims are withdrawn and are not reinstated in what follows: the retatrutide hepatic dose
plateau, the GDF-15 receptor class differential, and the answerability of question 2. Section 4.9
states the first of those as withdrawn, with the arithmetic, because a reader who knows the trial will
look for it.

## 4.5 The unfavourable axis is the axis this corpus cannot measure

A net claim about biological age requires both directions. A score that can only improve is a measure
of what the drug does to the markers someone chose to report, and the choice of markers is not
neutral. The composite inclusion rule retains lean mass for exactly this reason, as the only axis on
which the class may cause harm, and states that a composite excluding the one domain where harm is
plausible is not a net score.

The corpus cannot support that design. Of the 34 markers that yield a pooled estimate, 30 move
favourably and four unfavourably, and of the four only one is distinguishable from no effect.

| Marker | Trials | Change (%) | 95 per cent CI | Prediction interval | Reading |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Resting heart rate | 10 | plus 2.7 | 0.7 to 4.7 | minus 2.4 to 7.8 | Unfavourable and distinguishable from no effect |
| Total lean mass | 2 | minus 5.0 | minus 29.8 to 19.7 | Not estimable | Unfavourable point estimate, interval spanning a third of the scale in both directions |
| Body fat percentage | 2 | plus 0.2 | minus 9.9 to 10.2 | Not estimable | Nominally unfavourable, indistinguishable from nothing |
| Fibrosis improvement | 2 | minus 0.2 | minus 807.5 to 807.1 | Not estimable | Not a result; see decision C1 |
| BMD | 1 | plus 1.2 | 0.2 to 2.2 | Not estimable | Bone mineral density. Favourable, single trial, interval is that trial's own |

Two trials yield a poolable lean mass contrast. Thirty six yield one for high sensitivity C reactive
protein. That asymmetry is a measurement choice rather than a biological fact, and it is the single most consequential
gap in this evidence base, because it falls on the one domain that could reverse the sign of a net
score.

The mechanistic question behind lean mass remains open in a way the pooled estimate cannot address.
Loss of lean tissue during a sustained energy deficit is expected; whether it is adaptive, meaning
proportional to fat loss and without functional cost, or maladaptive, meaning disproportionate and
accompanied by loss of strength or physical function, is the distinction that decides whether this
axis belongs in an aging framework at all. The two framings in the literature pull in opposite
directions: one treats the magnitude of lean mass loss in this class as comparable to a decade or more
of aging, the other distinguishes adaptive from maladaptive muscle change and notes that the available
measurements cannot separate them. A network meta-analysis across 262 trials and 99,791 participants
reports tirzepatide producing both the largest fat mass reduction and the largest lean mass reduction
in the class, which is the pattern a reader would expect if lean loss tracks total loss, and which
still does not establish whether function follows.

What this review can state is narrow and worth stating precisely. It can report that the only
unfavourable signal distinguishable from no effect across 34 markers is a rise in resting heart rate of
2.7 per cent, and that the musculoskeletal axis is measured in two of 76 trials and therefore cannot
be evaluated. It cannot state that the class spares lean mass, and it cannot state that the class
harms it. The honest formulation is that the question is unanswerable from published data, and that
the reason is reporting rather than biology: body composition is measured by imaging in a substantial
minority of these trials and reported in a way that yields a poolable contrast in two of them.

## 4.6 From disease specific endpoints to a hallmark framing, and what the shift costs

The endpoints that organise this literature are disease specific: glycated haemoglobin, body weight,
and in the cardiovascular outcome trials a composite of major adverse cardiac events. A biological age
framing asks a different question of the same trials, namely whether the intervention moves the
hallmark processes of aging across domains rather than one disease axis within one domain. The shift
is legitimate and it has a price, which this review is in a position to quantify rather than assert.

The inventory declares 36 markers across 14 hallmark domains. Thirty five appear in at least one
trial, 34 yield a pooled estimate, and 26 are pooled over three or more trials. Epigenetic age is the
single inventory marker with no eligible row at all, and the one trial reporting it is held pending the
eligibility decision on the HIV associated lipohypertrophy population, which is decision B1.

Coverage is not evenly distributed across the domains; it tracks what routine trial chemistry already
produces.

| Coverage tier | Markers | Trials reporting |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Routine chemistry and vitals | hsCRP, HbA1c, fasting glucose, triglycerides, systolic pressure, diastolic pressure, total and LDL cholesterol | 40, 38, 35, 30, 30, 28, 27, 27 |
| Measured where the trial has a reason | VAT, adiponectin, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, ALT, TNF alpha, liver fat by MRI-PDFF, leptin | 7 to 19 |
| One or two trials | Bone mineral density, YKL-40, soluble ICAM-1, mid regional pro-ANP, the fatty liver index, MASH resolution, total lean mass, body fat percentage, fibrosis improvement | 1 or 2 |
| None | Epigenetic age | 0 eligible rows |

The structure of that table is the finding. The domains this drug class plausibly acts on through
energy balance and glycaemic control are the domains that regulatory trials already measure, so they
are well covered. The domains that would distinguish an aging effect from a metabolic one, meaning
epigenetic, mitochondrial and musculoskeletal, are measured in nought to three trials. The evidence
base is therefore shaped by what trials measure for approval, not by what the aging question needs, and
a hallmark signature cannot be read from it: five of the 14 domains rest on a single inventory marker,
namely autonomic aging, endothelial aging, epigenetic aging, mitochondrial stress and renal aging, and
in two of those five that marker is carried by one trial or by none.

A second cost is visible in the pooled counts themselves. The pooled number of trials for a marker is
at or below the number reporting it, because a trial that reports a marker with no dispersion, no
interval and no exact p value yields no percent scale quantity, and neither does a contrast whose
baseline denominator is nowhere printed. Thirty five trial level reports across the panel are lost this
way, four of them for high sensitivity C reactive protein and five for fasting glucose. Those are
measurements that were taken, at cost, and that cannot enter a synthesis because of how they were
printed.

The transferable contribution of this section is the inventory rather than the pooled estimates. A
declared panel of 36 markers, each with its hallmark domain, its direction of aging, its clinical
weight and its surrogacy grade, is reusable by the next review and by trialists choosing which markers
to add to a protocol. It also makes the gaps auditable: the gate chain fails when a pooled marker has no
inventory entry or an inventory entry is unaccounted for, which is how the epigenetic age gap is a
recorded absence rather than a silence.

What this review must not state is that moving these markers is moving biological age. The only direct
evidence that an agent in this class modulates a validated epigenetic clock comes from one drug in one
trial, and that trial sits in a population the review has not yet decided to include.

## 4.7 What a metabolic age normalisation threshold would require

Question 5 asks what thresholds would define metabolic age normalisation sufficient to justify
structured treatment discontinuation. The prespecified answer is a framework rather than numbers, and
this section reports it as such, because four requirements are missing simultaneously: age referenced
distributions for these markers in a comparable population, evidence that reaching a threshold predicts
outcome, off treatment biomarker trajectories, and a trial that randomises discontinuation on a
biomarker threshold. None exists.

The structural precedent is the consensus definition of type 2 diabetes remission, glycated
haemoglobin below 48 mmol per mol sustained at least three months after stopping glucose lowering
therapy. That definition works because a single marker captures the construct, the marker has an agreed
diagnostic threshold, and relapse is observable and clinically meaningful. None of the three holds
here: the construct is multi domain by definition, no marker in the inventory has an agreed threshold
for aging, and biomarker rebound is observable while its meaning is unknown. The analogy shows what a
normalisation definition would have to look like, not what its thresholds should be.

Four conditions follow, and the corpus now says something about each.

| Condition | What the corpus adds |
| --- | --- |
| C1. Each represented hallmark domain must reach its own criterion, rather than a composite crossing a line | The composite's own behaviour is the argument. Under inverse variance weighting one marker took 85 per cent of the weight in the earlier specification, and in the current per class composite the GLP-1 mono cell under inverse variance weighting carries a bootstrap bias of plus 2.50 on an estimate of 5.39, meaning the value depends materially on which of its nine admitted markers fall in a resample. A composite can be dragged across a threshold by one strongly moving marker |
| C2. Thresholds must be attainment of a value referenced to an age matched distribution, not a percentage change from baseline | This review pools percentage change, which is precisely the quantity that cannot define normalisation. A fall of 40 per cent from a very high starting value may not reach a normal range; a fall of 10 per cent from near normal may already be inside it. The reference distributions are not assembled here and are the first concrete piece of work the framework requires |
| C3. Only markers validated against outcomes may carry a threshold | On the surrogacy appraisal that is hsCRP, IL-6 and NT-proBNP, and of those the class meaningfully moves only hsCRP, at minus 31.1 per cent. hsCRP is also one of the two markers in the whole panel whose prediction interval excludes no effect, the other being HOMA-IR |
| C4. Normalisation must be sustained off treatment, within a defined observation window | Off treatment data exist for body weight alone. No off treatment aging biomarker data exist for any glucagon receptor containing agent, so no observation window can be specified and this condition cannot be operationalised at all |

The realistic contribution is therefore to specify the evidence a discontinuation decision would need,
so that trials now being designed can generate it. Three pieces are missing and each is tractable: age
referenced distributions for validated aging markers, which cohort consortia could supply now; off
treatment biomarker trajectories, which a discontinuation substudy in any ongoing phase 3 programme
could supply cheaply; and mediation analyses for markers beyond hsCRP, which secondary analyses of
completed cardiovascular outcome trials with stored samples could supply without new recruitment.

This section offers no numeric threshold. A figure offered against this evidence base would be an
expert opinion wearing the formatting of a result, and would be cited as a threshold long after the
caveats had been forgotten. The section is labelled hypothesis generating in the protocol and is
reported that way here.

## 4.8 Implications for geroscience trial design

The distance between what this corpus contains and what a geroscience claim requires is a design gap
rather than a biological one, and the useful feature of a design gap is that it can be closed by
additions to trials that are already running. Four asks follow from the sections above, each stated
against what these trials already do.

**Measure the unfavourable axis.** Two of 76 trials yield a poolable lean mass contrast, against 36 for
high sensitivity C reactive protein. Body composition imaging is already performed in a substantial
minority of these programmes, and the cost of reporting a change with its dispersion in a trial that has
already scanned its participants is close to nothing. Until that changes, no synthesis in this field
can report a net effect, because the one domain that could carry a negative sign is measured in a
rounding error of the evidence base.

**Report dispersion, and print the baseline.** Thirty five trial level reports across this panel are
lost because a marker appears without a standard deviation, a standard error, an interval or an exact p
value, or because the contrast is expressed as a percentage with the baseline denominator nowhere in
the paper. Four of the lost reports are for hsCRP and five for fasting glucose, markers whose
measurement was not in doubt. This is the cheapest of the four asks and the one that returns most: the
measurements exist and are already paid for.

**Add a discontinuation substudy.** Off treatment trajectories exist for body weight and for nothing
else. A substudy that measures a small validated panel at three and six months after planned
discontinuation, in a programme that is already following participants, would convert condition C4 of
the normalisation framework from unoperationalisable into testable. No such data exist for any
glucagon receptor containing agent.

**Pair the panel with a functional measure or a clock, in the randomised comparison.** One abstract in
the included pool pairs high sensitivity C reactive protein in 292 participants and interleukin 6 in
270 with a self reported physical function instrument, and reports that greater improvement in either
marker accompanied greater improvement in function, with the association for one domain surviving
adjustment for weight loss and the other not. It contributes no row to this synthesis, because the
analysis is by quartile of marker improvement rather than by randomised arm. The pairing therefore
exists in the evidence base without being usable, and the fix is an analysis choice rather than a new
measurement.

The common structure of the four asks is worth naming because it is the argument this review is
positioned to make. Each is an addition to an existing trial, not a new trial: one more scan reported,
one more column in a table, one more visit in a programme that already has visits, one more analysis
stratified by arm rather than by quartile. A field that wants to claim effects on aging will not get
there by running a dedicated geroscience trial first; it will get there by making the trials it is
already running answerable.

A note on reporting rather than design, since this review had to build the apparatus to find out what
it could say. The numbers in this paper are produced by a version controlled pipeline whose outputs
are hashed, whose figures cannot be reported unless the files exist, and whose provenance chain records
for every extracted value whether it was located in publisher text or in a capture made while reading a
rendered page. That apparatus was not built for elegance. It was built because, during this review, a
pooled interval was printed that excluded its own point estimate, a figure set was reported as produced
for five days while no figure existed, and seven of 29 anchor references cited an issue or page range
belonging to a different article in the right journal. None of those was found by reading the output.
Each was found by a check that compared the output with the thing it claimed to describe. A field
assembling biomarker panels across dozens of trials should expect the same class of error and should
expect to find it the same way.

## 4.9 The triple agonist case, and why the most informative molecule is the least informative here

Retatrutide is the molecule the field expects to be most informative about receptor class effects. It
produces the largest weight loss in the class and engages a third receptor, so it is the natural test of
whether a hepatic or inflammatory effect can be separated from the weight loss that accompanies it. In
this corpus it is the least informative molecule, and the reasons are about reporting rather than
biology.

What the triple agonist class contributes, in full: one trial, one biomarker, eight rows.

| Series | Dose, mg | Liver fat change against placebo (%) | Note |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Primary endpoint, least squares mean relative change | 1 | minus 43.2 | Enters the corpus |
| | 4 | minus 57.3 | |
| | 8 | minus 81.7 | |
| | 12 | minus 82.7 | Survives the shared control collapse |
| Later timepoint | 1 | minus 46.7 | MRI missing for 56.1 per cent of participants |
| | 4 | minus 54.4 | |
| | 8 | minus 77.1 | |
| | 12 | minus 81.4 | |

The class therefore has no pooled estimate for any marker, no composite, and no computable pairwise
contrast against any other class. One arm survives the collapse to one arm per trial per biomarker at
the highest dose, the 12 mg primary endpoint at minus 82.7 per cent with a mean body weight change of
minus 17.6 per cent, and a single arm does not pool. In the signature heatmap the triple agonist column
is present and empty throughout, which is the correct depiction and a more useful one than omitting the
column.

One disclosure belongs here rather than in a methods footnote. The collapse rule keeps the highest dose
arm per trial per biomarker and breaks ties without a stated rule, so the choice between the two 12 mg
rows was made by their order in the file. It happened to keep the primary endpoint rather than the
timepoint at which imaging was missing for 56.1 per cent of participants, which is the right outcome
reached by the wrong mechanism. The rule that would make it a decision is the timepoint question
recorded as D-X3 in the decision register, and this is the concrete case on which it turns.

**The dose plateau claim is withdrawn and is not reinstated.** An earlier draft argued that the near
identical effect at 8 and 12 mg, while weight loss continues to increase across that interval, is a dose
response feature arguing against pure weight mediation. The arithmetic does not support the inference.
Within the trial the difference between the 4 mg and 8 mg arms is 24.4 percentage points of relative
liver fat reduction, 2.2 to 46.6, p 0.032, while the difference between 8 mg and 12 mg is 1.0 point,
minus 16.7 to plus 18.7, p 0.91. Two arms at the top of a dose range, both near the floor of what the
measurement can register once most of the liver fat is gone, cannot distinguish a plateau in the
mechanism from a ceiling in the outcome. The second comparison is an absence of evidence in two dose
arms of one trial.

What is absent from the triple agonist arm is not a random subset of the panel. GDF-15, high
sensitivity C reactive protein, interleukin 6, adiponectin, leptin, epigenetic clocks and biopsy
confirmed steatohepatitis histology are all missing. Those are, with one exception, precisely the
markers that would separate an aging effect from a weight effect, which is the question this molecule is
best placed to answer because it produces the largest weight loss. The molecule with the strongest
mechanistic case carries the thinnest biomarker panel in the corpus.

The consequence is not confined to this section. The weight adjusted hepatic meta-regression rests on
seven arms, of which the triple agonist contributes one, and in that model the class with the largest
weight losses is represented by a single point. Class and weight change are therefore nearly collinear,
which is why the adjusted model cannot separate them and why the weight coefficient reverses sign on
adjustment. The single marker triple agonist class is the direct cause of the identifiability problem
reported in section 4.4, and a targeted retrieval of the main publications behind this arm, recorded as
decision B5, is the one piece of further retrieval in this project that could change an analysis rather
than a count.

Stated plainly, so that it is not mistaken for a negative finding about the drug: this review reports
no receptor class signature for the triple agonist because the published evidence contains one
biomarker from one trial for that class, not because the molecule was found to lack an effect. The
distinction matters for anyone reading the signature heatmap, and it is the reason the empty column is
drawn.
