# 2. Methods

Reconstructed 16 October 2026 from the version controlled protocol and amendment record
(`prespecification/`, `docs/`). Every statement here is traceable to a file in this repository and to
the commit that fixed it, which is the only basis on which a reader can check that a choice preceded
the estimates it governs.

## 2.1 Registration and protocol

The review follows a protocol fixed on 14 October 2026, before any trial was extracted, and recorded
in `prespecification/analysis_plan.md`. The analysis pipeline was written against that protocol
before the extraction file existed. The ordering is not asserted but timestamped by the commit
history of the repository, which is the point of fixing it in public: no later choice of estimator,
moderator, weighting scheme or subgroup could be made after the estimates were visible. The PROSPERO
record is `prespecification/PROSPERO_submission.md` with its field mapping in
`docs/PROSPERO_field_map.md`.

Five questions were fixed in advance and are unchanged.

| Question | Statement |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Which established aging biomarkers are modified by incretin and glucagon receptor containing peptide therapies, and in which direction |
| Q2 | Does the aging biomarker signature differ by receptor class |
| Q3 | Is the hepatic aging signal with GCGR containing agents independent of weight loss magnitude |
| Q4 | Can a composite score compare receptor classes on a single scale |
| Q5 | What thresholds would define metabolic age normalization sufficient to justify a structured discontinuation trial |

Four hypotheses were fixed with their directions, including one, H4, recorded as untestable with
available data so that it could not later be presented as a prediction the corpus had confirmed.
Q3 is reported throughout as an association and not as a mechanism, and Q2 is reported with the
coverage limitation stated in section 2.11.

## 2.2 Receptor class assignment

Classes follow the molecule's receptor pharmacology, not the trial's framing or its indication.

| Class | Molecules admitted |
|---|---|
| GLP1_mono | semaglutide, liraglutide, exenatide, dulaglutide |
| GLP1_GIP | tirzepatide |
| GLP1_GCGR | survodutide, mazdutide, pemvidutide, cotadutide |
| GLP1_GIP_GCGR | retatrutide |

The admission of exenatide and dulaglutide as interventions is a post hoc widening of a published
eligibility criterion and is disclosed as such in section 2.12. Amylin containing combinations
(cagrilintide, CagriSema, amycretin) are staged but not pooled, pending a decision on whether an
amylin receptor constitutes a fifth class rather than a modifier of an existing one.

## 2.4 Screening and adjudication

Records were screened in two stages, title and abstract followed by full text, with each record
assessed independently twice and disagreements adjudicated rather than resolved by majority. All 149
full text disagreements were adjudicated and no record remains pending at either stage. The flow and
its counts are in `docs/PRISMA_FLOW.md` and reported as a PRISMA diagram.

One retrieval limitation is disclosed rather than corrected. The search strategy requires a biomarker
term, so a trial whose abstract reports only weight and glycated haemoglobin was not retrieved even
where its full publication reports aging biomarkers. This was identified for the two retatrutide
phase 2 parent publications, whose marker evidence consequently enters the review only through a
later abstract. A trial level backfill by registry number would correct it and would change the
PRISMA identification count, so it is recorded as an amendment candidate and not applied silently.

## 2.5 Operational biomarker inventory

Q1 is phrased against a prespecified inventory of 26 markers, reported as Table 1. The markers
actually pooled drifted from that list in both directions during extraction, and the review reports
the inventory as it was pooled rather than as Table 1 reads, following amendment R2 of 15 October.
Two markers were added by that amendment with their domain, direction and provisional clinical
weight declared at the time of addition: tumour necrosis factor alpha, to the inflammaging domain,
and mid regional pro atrial natriuretic peptide, to the cardiac aging domain. Neither enters the
eleven marker composite inclusion rule; both appear in the pooled table and in the all markers
sensitivity analysis. Five markers reported by included trials were not admitted because they fall
outside any aging hallmark domain named in the protocol.

A marker enters the master only if it appears in Table 1 or in the amendment record with those three
properties declared. This rule exists because the drift it replaced was undocumented.

## 2.6 Data extraction

One row per trial, arm and biomarker, against the schema in `analysis/data/extraction_schema.csv`
and the extractor brief in `docs/EXTRACTION_BRIEF.md`, which carries fourteen numbered amendments
(A1 to A14) each recording the case that prompted it.

This literature reports between group contrasts with intervals far more often than arm level means
with standard deviations, and the extraction records whichever the source printed, together with the
route by which a standard error was obtained. Routes are named on every row and include a reported
interval, a reported standard error, an exact p value, arm standard errors of the mean, arm standard
deviations, event counts on a log risk or odds scale, and the imputation described next. A row
without a declared route is refused by the build.

Three extraction rules govern recurring structures in this literature and were fixed when first
encountered rather than per trial.

**R1, parallel group trials reporting baseline and final arm means with standard deviations but
nothing about within patient change.** A contrast of final values reproduces any chance baseline
imbalance as a treatment effect, which in one 24 patient trial turned within arm changes with p
values of 0.99 and 0.57 into a final value comparison at p = 0.017. The contrast is therefore the
difference of mean changes, and the standard deviation of change in each arm is imputed from the
reported baseline and final standard deviations under an assumed pre post correlation r, following
the Cochrane Handbook:

    sd_change = sqrt(sd_base^2 + sd_final^2 - 2 r sd_base sd_final)

In words, the variance of a change is the sum of the variances at the two timepoints less twice their
covariance, so the more strongly a patient's baseline predicts their final value, the smaller the
variability of the change. The primary analysis assumes r = 0.5, recorded on every row that uses this
route, and the build refuses such a row without it. A lower correlation yields a larger change
standard deviation and a wider interval, so if 0.5 overstates the true correlation the primary
interval is too narrow; reruns at r = 0.3 and r = 0.7 are therefore mandatory and reported.

**R3, crossover trials.** The contrast is the authors' own within patient treatment difference with
its reported interval or exact p value, never recomputed from arm level standard deviations, which
would discard the pairing. Where a trial reports a median of paired differences, the row carries the
approximation to a mean difference in its notes and is a candidate for the influence analysis.

**A12, median baselines.** A percent conversion requires a baseline mean. For markers whose
distribution is strongly skewed a median baseline is not an acceptable denominator, and rows whose
only available denominator is a median stay in native units rather than being converted. This leaves
38 rows outside the percent scale pooling, which is reported as a limitation rather than worked
around.

## 2.7 Effect measures and harmonisation

The protocol specifies Hedges' g for continuous outcomes, the standardised mean difference with the
small sample correction. That measure requires a mean, a standard deviation and a sample size in both
arms. Those quantities are present on 160 of the 524 extracted rows and complete on fewer, because
the trials in this corpus publish a between group contrast with an interval. The review therefore
pools percent change in the biomarker rather than Hedges' g, which is a deviation from the registered
plan and is disclosed in section 2.12.

Each row's native effect is converted to the percent change scale by a declared route and the route
is recorded. A row reporting a mean difference in absolute units is divided by a baseline mean, which
is keyed by trial and biomarker with the table and the arithmetic that produced it. A row already
reporting a relative change is taken directly. A row reporting a ratio of geometric means or a risk
or odds ratio is converted on the log scale. Where no admissible denominator exists the row remains
in native units and does not enter the percent scale pooling.

For a biomarker reported as a percent change in each arm, the between group contrast is not the
arithmetic difference of the two percentages. A percent change is a ratio, so the contrast is

    contrast = (1 + t/100) / (1 + c/100) - 1

expressed as a percentage, where t and c are the percent changes in the treatment and comparator
arms. In words, each arm's percent change describes a multiplicative factor applied to its own
baseline, and the between group effect is the ratio of those two factors rather than their
difference. The two forms diverge in proportion to how far the comparator arm moved; on one high
sensitivity C reactive protein contrast in this corpus they differ by 8.4 percentage points on a 42
per cent effect.

Binary outcomes, meaning MASH resolution and fibrosis improvement, use the log odds ratio with a
continuity correction applied only to zero cells, and are back transformed for presentation.

## 2.8 Unit of analysis, and the shared control problem

Several trials contribute more than one dose arm against a single shared comparator arm. Pooling
those arms as though they were independent studies counts the comparator patients more than once and
narrows every interval that includes them. The synthesis therefore retains one arm per trial per
biomarker, the highest dose, and the discarded arms are counted and reported. Across the corpus this
collapses 483 analysable rows to 389, dropping 94 dose arms.

This rule is not in the registered plan, which assumed one contrast per trial, and it is disclosed in
section 2.12. Its effect is visible in the pooled k of every biomarker: without it, liver fat would
pool 18 contrasts rather than 9 and glycated haemoglobin 44 rather than 38.

Within trial arms belonging to different receptor classes are at present also removed by the highest
dose rule, which is recorded as an open item rather than a settled choice.

## 2.9 Synthesis

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Primary estimator | Random effects, restricted maximum likelihood |
| Sensitivity estimators | DerSimonian and Laird, Paule and Mandel, Sidik and Jonkman |
| Small sample correction | Hartung, Knapp, Sidik and Jonkman where fewer than 10 trials contribute |
| Uncertainty | A confidence interval on every pooled estimate, and a prediction interval wherever the between study variance is estimable, which requires at least three trials |
| Primary heterogeneity statistic | tau squared, with I squared reported for comparability only |
| Meta regression | biomarker on receptor class and percentage weight change, with the interaction, in four nested models, with permutation inference at 10,000 iterations |
| Model identifiability | Fitted only where contributing trials exceed estimated parameters by at least three |
| Influence | Leave one out on every estimate with at least three trials |
| Small study effects | Egger regression and trim and fill only where 10 or more trials contribute, otherwise reported as not assessable |
| Seed | 20261013 |

A confidence interval describes uncertainty about the average effect; a prediction interval describes
the range in which the effect in a new trial would be expected to fall. The second is wider and is
the quantity a clinician should read, which is why it is reported on every estimate where it can be
computed and why its absence below three trials is stated rather than left blank.

The composite score is a weighted mean of direction adjusted effects normalised by the sum of the
weights, so that a score computed over few markers is not inflated relative to one computed over
many. Equal weighting is reported as primary and inverse variance weighting as sensitivity. This
reverses the order named in the protocol, for a reason recorded when the reversal was made: inverse
variance weighting across biomarkers weights each marker by how precisely it happened to be
measured, and in this corpus a single trial's C reactive protein measurement took 85 per cent of the
weight. Both schemes are reported in every run and the protocol's ranking stability rule applies to
both. Composite intervals use a two level bootstrap at 10,000 resamples, resampling biomarkers and
perturbing each estimate by its own standard error, so that both the choice of markers and the
imprecision of each contribute to the interval. Class contrasts are bootstrapped directly and
restricted to biomarkers measured in both classes.

## 2.10 Risk of bias and prespecified sensitivity analyses

Risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2 and 77 records were completed. Nine sensitivity analyses were
fixed in advance: drug level against class level network; type 2 diabetes against non diabetes; MASH
against non MASH; dose stratification within class; trial duration in three bands; industry against
non industry funding; DXA only against combined body composition methods; exclusion of trials at high
risk of bias; and the four estimator sweep. Three further analyses were added by amendment with their
reasons: the pre post correlation reruns at r = 0.3 and 0.7 required by R1, and a re pooling of every
marker touched by an exenatide or dulaglutide row with those rows removed, which shows the reader the
pooled table under the eligibility criterion as originally published.

## 2.11 Provenance verification

Every number in the synthesis is checked by an automated chain of twenty five gates, recorded in
`claude/M5-AGE-provenance-gate-chain-v1.1.md` and executable as `scripts/run_gates.sh`. Eight gates
validate the pipeline against its own declarations and recover known parameters from simulated data.
Seven check the corpus: that every pooled figure written in the narrative is the figure the pipeline
produced; that no packet bundles two unrelated studies, which would silently drop the study nobody
read; that each extracted number is attached to its own biomarker in its source rather than to a
neighbouring table row; that a row's printed p value reconciles with the p implied by its own effect
and standard error; that no two trials report the same run of values, which is how a duplicate
publication enters a corpus twice; that a row's arm values and its reported effect agree in sign and
magnitude, which is the signature of a swapped arm; and what the preceding gates are entitled to
establish.

That last gate sets a limit on the others and is reported here rather than omitted. The sources the
gates read are of two kinds: text as the publisher printed it, and captures written during this review
by reading a rendered page that could not be retrieved as text. Against the first, agreement is
independent verification that the extraction reproduces the source. Against the second, agreement
establishes that the extraction reproduces the capture, and a misreading at the capture step would be
reproduced rather than caught. Of the 524 rows, 295 have their numbers located in publisher text, 101
in a capture alone, 12 in both, four in a conference abstract or registry entry alone, and 112 in no
retained source, the last figure reconciling with the attachment gate's own counts. The 101 are not
suspect rows and nothing indicates any is wrong; the limitation is on what the verification proves
for them, and it is being reduced by retrieving publisher text for those trials.

Seven further gates ask whether the records this manuscript rests on still describe the data beside
them. They check that the PRISMA stages reconcile, each record at a stage being accounted for by the
stage above it; that every pooled biomarker has an inventory entry and every inventory entry is
accounted for, including the one marker in the inventory that no trial reports, and that the
published inventory table is byte for byte what that inventory and the harmonised master produce,
compared rather than regenerated so that a disagreement is reported instead of overwritten; that the
analysis pipeline's primary pooling reproduces the independently written pooling scripts to the last decimal
for all 28 shared biomarkers; that every composite interval contains the estimate it qualifies; that
the reference list covers every trial in the corpus and that each non verified citation is written up
rather than left blank; and that every figure the submission will carry exists on disk, is hashed in
the result manifest, and is mapped to a submission number; and that every digest in that
manifest still matches the file beside it, so that the manifest is a check rather than a list. The last three were written after each
caught a defect that the preceding fifteen could not see: an interval printed around a quantity
other than the one it qualified, a reference list that had drifted from a corrected master, and a
figure set that had never existed because the graphics device the pipeline requested could not load
and reported a warning rather than an error. Each of those three runs with a self test that injects
the defect it was written for, on the same principle the arm orientation gate follows: a gate that
reports nothing has not thereby shown that it works.

Two gates address the one blind spot the others cannot reach. In 122 of the 524 rows the between
group effect was not published and was computed from the two arm values, so the gate that compares a
row's arms against its reported effect has nothing to compare: a swapped arm flips the derived effect
with it and the two agree by construction. Both gates therefore go back to the source text and ask
which arm the paper attributes each value to. The first reads the arm name immediately preceding a
value, which is how narrative results are written, and attributes 8 rows. The second reads the header
above a table of values and applies the order in which that header names the arms, which is how
table captures are written, and attributes 15. Neither finds a swapped arm. The remaining 99 rows are
reported as unattributable with the reason for each. For 50 the two values never appear on the same
line of any retained source, for 6 both are preceded by the same arm's name, and for 3 the two arms
print the same value, so no attribution is possible. Of the 55 rows the second gate examines, one of
the two values coincides with a dispersion rather than a column value in 15, the two values are not
adjacent columns on any line that carries both in 14, which is what a three arm table looks like,
the nearest line naming both arms names one of them more than once in 6, which is a dose split
column list, no line within reach names both arms in 4, and the nearest line naming both arms is
prose rather than a row of column labels in 1. The limit of 23 attributed rows is
stated here because the alternative is to present a verification of the derived rows that has not
taken place. Six of the 23 rest on a caption this review wrote rather than on publisher text, and for
those the gate establishes that the extraction matches the capture and not that it matches the
printed table, the same distinction the provenance gate draws for the corpus as a whole.

A final gate compares the review's record against the trial registries rather than against the
papers. For each row the extraction marks as the trial's prespecified endpoint, it asks whether the
timepoint the row was read at is the timepoint the trial registered as its primary endpoint, reading
the ClinicalTrials.gov record of each of the 44 included trials that carries a registry identifier.
A registered time frame is read as a visit or as a window according to its own wording, because six
of the first ten disagreements it reported were rows read at 36 months inside a window registered as
up to 4.5 years. Of the 524 rows, 234 reach a verdict and sit at a registered primary endpoint or
inside a registered window, 4 are flagged as their paper's prespecified endpoint at a timepoint the
registration does not carry, 53 are unflagged and sit elsewhere, which carries no expectation, and
233 are out of reach: 143 belong to the 32 trials with no registry identifier, 68 carry no
timepoint, and 22 have a registered time frame stated in words rather than in figures. The 4 are not
extraction errors and are recorded individually: one is a magnetic resonance substudy reporting at
its own 24 week endpoint within a trial registered on HbA1c at 48 weeks, and three are inflammatory
markers reported at 18 months in a trial registered on tissue oxygen pressure at 6 months. They are
the measured size of a distinction the field's name conceals, since the extraction records an
endpoint as prespecified when either the paper or its registration names it so, and this review's
discussion of reporting bias rests on that field.

Three integrity exclusions were made at extraction rather than at eligibility, each recorded with the
arithmetic that justified it: a trial whose printed paired p values are impossible for its printed
means and standard deviations; a trial whose post treatment values duplicate those of an earlier
paper in the same population; and a trial whose baseline comparisons are inconsistent with the
randomisation it reports.

## 2.12 Deviations from the registered protocol

Stated together, because a reader checking a registered review is entitled to find them in one place
rather than inferring them from the analysis.

| Deviation | What changed | When and why it was recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Pooling scale | Percent change in the biomarker rather than Hedges' g | The standardised mean difference requires arm level means and standard deviations, which this literature does not publish for most contrasts. Recorded at the schema correction of 15 October; the registered analysis pipeline was ported onto the harmonised scale on 16 October and reproduces the reported pooled table to within 0.001 on all 28 shared biomarkers |
| Unit of analysis | One arm per trial per biomarker, highest dose retained | Dose arms share a comparator, so pooling them as independent studies overstates precision. Absent from the plan, which assumed one contrast per trial |
| Eligibility | Exenatide and dulaglutide admitted as interventions, not only as comparators | The plan excluded them on indication and era; its own principle, that class follows receptor pharmacology, admits them as selective GLP-1 receptor agonists. Recorded on the day it was noticed, after three such trials had been read and before any class contrast involving them was interpreted. A sensitivity analysis reports the pooled table under the original criterion |
| Composite weighting | Equal weighting reported as primary, inverse variance as sensitivity | Inverse variance across biomarkers weights by measurement precision rather than importance; one trial's C reactive protein took 85 per cent of the weight |
| Biomarker inventory | Two markers added, five reported markers not admitted, fourteen Table 1 markers not yet extracted | Amendment R2, which replaced undocumented drift with a declared inventory |
| Prediction intervals | Suppressed below three contributing trials | A between study variance resting on a single degree of freedom does not support an interval a reader should act on |

## 2.13 Threats acknowledged in advance

**Ecological fallacy.** Trial level slopes can differ in magnitude and in sign from within patient
relationships. Every conclusion is framed about drug classes and never about individuals.

**Overadjustment.** Weight loss plausibly lies on the causal path from receptor engagement to
biomarker improvement, so conditioning on it removes part of the effect being estimated. The adjusted
estimate is read as a conservative lower bound and the unadjusted coefficient is reported beside it.

**Undetectable reporting bias.** Aging biomarkers appear in this literature predominantly in industry
post hoc analyses rather than as prespecified endpoints. Selective reporting in that setting is not
detectable by funnel asymmetry, because results that were never reported do not enter the literature
as small imprecise studies. Whether each contrast was a prespecified endpoint is extracted so that
the extent of post hoc reporting is at least visible. No method available here resolves it.

## 2.14 Reporting rules fixed in advance

**Fragility.** A pooled estimate whose direction or statistical significance reverses on removal of a
single trial is reported as fragile and is not advanced as a conclusion, whatever its nominal p value.

**Ranking stability.** The composite ranking of receptor classes is reported as a finding only where
it is stable across all weighting schemes. Where the ranking changes with the weighting, that
instability is itself the result.

**Class level pooling.** If the drug level and class level networks disagree materially, the class
level simplification is reported as invalidated rather than preferred.

**Coverage disclosure.** Any composite score or contrast is reported with the number of contributing
biomarkers stated alongside it. A score computed over seven biomarkers is not presented as
commensurate with one computed over sixteen.
